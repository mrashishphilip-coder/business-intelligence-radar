AI-native products can own the work between systems

A decision-grade synthesis designed to replace 60–90 minutes of passive listening with 10–15 minutes of active understanding.
~12 min read
Current thesis

The next enterprise software boundary is “system of action,” not another system of record.

Legacy applications capture the final state. AI-native products can increasingly own the reasoning, coordination and exception handling required to produce that state.

Novelty 9.4Evidence 8.7Confidence 8.6Relevance 9.8
The argument

1. Records compress the real workflow

An ERP row hides negotiation, interpretation, internal approval, exceptions and judgment. That invisible work is the opportunity surface.

2. Agents change the product boundary

Instead of helping a human complete each step, the product can accept an objective and execute the workflow under policy constraints.

3. Trust compounds gradually

Human review is not merely a safety layer. It becomes data about how the organization makes decisions, allowing controlled expansion of autonomy.

Counterarguments

Incumbents already own distribution

Salesforce, SAP and Oracle can embed agentic capability into existing products and customer relationships.

Autonomy may be overestimated

Many enterprise processes have sparse data, ambiguous policy and asymmetric downside. Humans may remain central longer than optimistic narratives imply.

Integration can become the moat

If execution still depends on dozens of incumbent APIs, the agent layer may struggle to capture all economics.

What would change my mind
  • Low sustained adoption once human review is removed.
  • Incumbents demonstrate materially faster deployment due to installed-base advantage.
  • Customers refuse outcome-based pricing and continue preferring per-seat tools.
Implications
  • Product: start from the job and failure modes, not the chatbot interface.
  • GTM: sell measurable workflow outcomes.
  • Architecture: permissions, observability and human escalation become core product primitives.
  • Strategy: identify work hidden outside systems of record.
